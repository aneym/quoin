The Hive That Kept Its Own Books¶

Marta ran the north apiary for eleven seasons before she let anyone else touch the ledger. Every comb she pulled got a line: the hive number, the weight of the frame, the color of the pollen coming in, and a note on temperament, because a calm box one week could turn mean the next. She kept the pages in a tin under the extractor, not because she distrusted the cooperative's shared binder, but because paper near honey survives spills better than anything digital does. When new keepers joined the yard, she handed them the tin before she handed them a smoker, insisting the numbers taught patience faster than the bees ever would.

Note

Swarm season entries get double-checked before filing; a mistimed split costs more than a mislabeled jar of honey.
