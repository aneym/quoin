Governing question: when a stranger describes something expensive that went wrong, how
does the reader turn that into a bounded, paid, first engagement, and what remains when
it ends?

**Movement IV — deliver and hand off**
8. Qureshi, "Reflections on Palantir" (nabeelqu.substack.com), **section 2 only**
   ("Forward deployed"). DECIDED 2026-08-29: included. It carries a distinct first-client
   delivery lesson none of the retained sources cover: deliver a kernel of value fast so
   the customer knows you are real, overfit the solution to the one problem and let
   generalization be someone else's job, and capture tacit context by working where the
   customer works. The services-to-product pivot, Foundry history, and margin material
   inside the same section are later-stage color; the interlude names them as such.
   Husain's field guide stays out per Alex's cut (expert overlap); held for a delivery
   companion volume.

Closing: final debrief prompt (spoken, unaided): describe the diagnostic you would sell
on Monday, its price, the evidence you would gather, and the one artifact the client
still uses in thirty days.
